## 3. Robot physical data and joint types

1. Mass, center of mass and inertia have independent completeness and source records. A scalar measured mass fills its mass field. The [point 06 source review](/home/leo/projects/3dmodeling/research/action-audit/reviews/point-06-sources.md) identifies OCCT `BRepGProp::VolumeProperties` as the integration step and `GProp_GProps` as its result/accumulator. Unit-density volume integrals require coherent physical density exactly once. The integration API assumes finite, closed, coherently oriented solids; the procedure checks those conditions first.
2. Tensor reference point, axes, units, component order and product/off-diagonal sign convention precede conversion. Volume scales with coordinate length cubed; geometric volume inertia scales with length to the fifth power. A physical inertia tensor uses mass times length squared. Rotation, parallel-axis shifts and component aggregation have explicit methods and future reference cases in the [action contracts](/home/leo/projects/3dmodeling/research/action-audit/point-06-operations.json).
3. Revolute, continuous and prismatic joints require their motion axis; planar joints require a plane normal. Fixed and floating joints have inapplicable axis fields. Continuous joints retain an axis while omitting finite travel bounds. The selected type and consumer determine limit, effort and velocity checks. The [independent review](/home/leo/projects/3dmodeling/research/action-audit/reviews/point-06-independent.md) records the corrected rule and future fixtures.
4. Robot visual geometry, contact geometry and mass-distribution geometry retain separate roles. Source and compiled mesh/frame mappings account for MuJoCo's mesh centering and principal-axis transforms. The source-to-compiled comparison records actual body fusion/defaults and geometry-driven inertia changes.
